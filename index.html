A map to think with

Walk through a real decision.

Bring the starting map into the conversation. Invite changes while following one decision the person knows well. A map prepared beforehand is optional.

45–60 minutesProposed guideRevise after first conversations
  1. Start with their role · 5 minutesWhat work are you responsible for? Which AI-related decision would help us understand how things happen?
  2. Correct the starting map · 10 minutesWhat looks right? Who or what is missing? Which connections would you change? Where do you see yourself in this process?
  3. Trace the decision · 15 minutesWho raised the issue? What happened next? Who could approve, fund, revise, pause, or limit it? Where did the process speed up or stall?
  4. Follow contributions and consequences · 10 minutesWhose input changed the plan? How did students, classified professionals, and faculty raise concerns? Who responded? What did the choice make possible or more difficult?
  5. Check the account · 5–10 minutesWhat documents would help us follow this? Who might describe it differently? What remains unsettled? What should we bring back for another look?

Keep disagreements between accounts visible. Distinguish what someone experienced, what documents establish, and what we infer. Revisit meaningful choices to use, limit, or decline AI and the consequences for others.

Sources & scope

This is a research instrument in development. Formal roles, documented relationships, and interview questions are kept distinct. The layout expresses organization, not a measured hierarchy of power.

Regional consortia are coordinating networks, not a management tier. The eight economic regions and the four 2026–27 ASCCC-area PD networks are separate structures. The map sketches typical local-district governance; Calbright’s statewide governance requires a separate treatment.

Named people are interview leads from a planning conversation. Their inclusion does not establish their role, influence, or agreement to participate. No sensitive accounts about individuals are included.

Research notes and added connections are stored in this browser. Export data to keep a copy or move between computers. This first version has no shared editing or automatic source updates.
